## Modelling decisions (for the teacher's comments)

1. **An LED is the classroom model:** a fixed forward voltage (red 2.0 · yellow 2.1 · green 2.2 · blue 3.0 · white
   3.0 V, screen 12b) plus a small series resistance (1 Ω), with a smooth knee so the solver converges. So the
   answer a student works out by hand, (3.3 − 2.0) / 220 = 5.9 mA, is what the app shows (screens 03, N-02).
   Rated 20 mA ("can take 20 mA"); burnt above 30 mA.
2. **A GPIO pin set HIGH is 3.3 V until its current limit.** Real pins are current-limited (that is why screen 04
   shows ≈ 48 mA, not hundreds). A plain resistance can't give both 5.9 mA through 220 Ω *and* 48 mA with no
   resistor, so a pin is a 3.3 V source with 1 Ω that saturates smoothly at its short-circuit current (Pico W 48 mA).
   Its *safe* limit (Pico W 12 mA) is what the warning uses.
3. **Supplies (3V3, VBUS, VIN, batteries) are the same model** with their own resistance and limit, so a short
   gives a large but finite current, and the rule *short* is: the supply is at its limit and its voltage has
   collapsed (or its + is wired straight to GND).
4. Values not published by the makers are **estimates**, marked `"estimate": true` in `board.json` and listed
   below, for the teacher to confirm.
