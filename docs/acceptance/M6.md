## Findings and decisions

0. **The teacher approved screens 17 and 17b** (2026-11-07) and left questions 1 and 2 below unanswered: the defaults stay
   (a new micro:bit project has the GPIO board's USB plugged in; green and blue dim on 3.3 V) until the teacher says otherwise.

1. **The RGB LED's green and blue are dim on 3.3 V through 220 Ω** (≈ 1.4 mA, 7 % of full), as on a real 5 mm RGB LED
   (green and blue ≈ 3.0–3.2 V). That is right, and worth a Learn note (M8): a smaller resistor (100 Ω) for green and
   blue. *For the teacher:* say if your RGB LEDs look different.
2. **The micro:bit GPIO board's 5V** works only while its own micro-USB is plugged in (board.json since M1). A project
   keeps whether it is (`usb`). *For the teacher:* a new micro:bit project starts with it **plugged in** (as before) —
   say if it should start unplugged, as a student's board usually is.
3. **A battery pack's leads go into breadboard holes** (usually a rail pair); with nothing joined to the board's GND it
   works on its own, as the battery-pack example shows.
4. **Where the light card goes**: under the sensor's breadboard (kept inside the canvas), not beside the sensor as
   drawn on screen 17 — beside it, it covered the next part (found in the app's screenshot). The card says the
   sensor's resistance; *the pin's voltage* (screen 17: "GP26 has 0.96 V") is left for the Plotter and the pin view
   (M8), which have the solved voltages.
5. **A knob turns with the drag up or down** (150 px from one end to the other), not round the knob: easier with a
   trackpad and the same on a turned part. Its position counts from leg 1, as on a real potentiometer turned clockwise.
6. **Buzzers are piezo buzzers.** A magnetic buzzer (16–42 Ω coil, the green-backed "passive buzzer" of some Arduino
   kits) would draw 80–200 mA from a pin — it needs a transistor (M7's parts, a Learn note in M8). *For the teacher:*
   say if your kit's buzzers are the magnetic kind.
7. **A passive buzzer follows hardware PWM** (`PWM(...)`, `music`), not a pin toggled by hand in a loop (bit-banged
   tones): that is a few kHz of pin changes the window can't follow. Rare in class; noted for M8's Learn page.
8. **The status bar's sound control** uses a speaker icon rather than screen 06's microphone, and says *Sound on (from
   the laptop)* (screen 06: *Speaker on*), as it covers buzzers on every board, not only the micro:bit's speaker.
9. **Screen 18 (M6.3 design, for the teacher)**: each sensor module stands in row f, its legs in holes, its board
   drawn up over the top half — so the other holes of its own columns (g–j) stay free for wires. The **PIR** is held
   with the mouse (like a push button) to move in front of it; it stays high 3 s after. The **line sensor** is
   clicked to slide it over the black line or white (a strip above its head shows which). The **soil sensor**'s
   moisture and the **TMP36**'s temperature are set on **one Inputs card** — with a row for every sensor that has a
   slider, the light sensor's too (replacing screen 17's card for each light sensor), so several sensors don't stack
   several cards. *For the teacher:* the pin orders are the common modules' — PIR **VCC OUT GND**, line sensor
   **OUT GND VCC**, soil sensor **AOUT VCC GND**, TMP36 / LM35 **+Vs Vout GND** — say if your kit's modules differ.
   The soil icon is now the black capacitive sensor (the prototype's was a two-prong resistive one).
10. **The micro:bit has no DHT example**: its MicroPython has no `dht` module (and its timing can't read one in
    Python). The weather station is on the Pico W and the ESP32. The micro:bit reads an HC-SR04 with
    `machine.time_pulse_us`, so it has the distance example.
11. **Screen 19 (M6.4 design, for the teacher)**: the DHT22 (white; a DHT11 is blue) and the GY-521 stand in the
    breadboard like screen 18's sensors (pins VCC DATA GND; VCC GND SCL SDA XDA XCL AD0 INT). The HC-SR04 is screen
    07's drawing with its four pins in holes — its board stands over them: at the breadboard's top edge it is turned
    (R) to face away, as a real one is plugged in; its sound waves show while it measures. The **Inputs** card gets a
    row for each: the DHT's temperature and humidity, the object's distance (2–400 cm, with its echo's length, as
    screen 07), the MPU6050's tilt about X and Y (−90…90°, with the g it reads) — the GY-521's level shows the tilt.
12. **The teacher approved screen 19** (2026-11-07) and asked for **every part of the SunFounder Universal Maker
    Sensor Kit** too (its 36 Arduino lessons, 39 items). The class doesn't use that kit: the parts are modelled on
    common modules, not on SunFounder's boards. Plan (the teacher's choice): finish slice 4 (screen 19) first; then a
    new slice 5 for the kit's inputs — 5a modules with a digital / analog output (16), 5b the chips (PCF8591, BMP280,
    DS18B20, DS1302, VL53L0X, MAX30102) — with a design screen before their UI; its outputs into M7; the ESP8266 and
    the JDY-31 Bluetooth into the IoT phase. The micro:bit's own sensors move to slice 6, the library pack to 7.
13. **On the micro:bit, a module's own pull-up meets the pin's pull-down** (CODAL's default, ≈ 13 kΩ): the level would
    sit half way. The examples say `set_pull(NO_PULL)` — a Learn note for M8. **The MQ-2's AO passes 3.3 V** from its
    5 V supply in gas (up to 3.5 V): the ADC reads its top; a divider keeps the whole range (Learn note).
