## Findings and decisions

1. **Fonts ship with the app** (`@fontsource-variable/inter`, `@fontsource-variable/jetbrains-mono`, OFL 1.1): the
   prototype loads them from Google Fonts, which the app can't (offline, N-04; its CSP allows `font-src 'self'`).
2. **Panel limits:** parts 220–360 px, code 320 px to half the window, the circuit at least 360 px; within these the
   widths are the saved proportions.
3. **Mutation checks (1b):** the stepper stepping by 1 instead of E12 → 2 fail; Esc not closing the dropdown → 3
   fail; arrows landing on disabled options → 3 fail.
4. **Tab stops:** the stepper's − / + are not Tab stops (the arrow keys do the same on its number), nor is a search
   field's × (Esc empties it); a radio group, segments and swatches are one Tab stop each. Disabled controls are
   never reached by Tab.
5. **The dropdown's list is `position: fixed`** under (or over) its field, so a panel that scrolls or clips (the
   inspector, M5) never cuts it off; it closes when the window is resized.
6. **Slice 2 reads and writes the `.mlab` file** (`format.ts`): opening needs the reader, and the tests need the
   writer. Slice 6 adds Save / Save as, changes not saved, and the starter-project round trip (F-60).
7. **New project… doesn't ask about changes yet** — nothing can be changed or saved before slices 3–6; slice 6 adds
   "Save your changes?" before the project is closed.
8. **Three board cards, as on screen 01.** The ESP32 with the extension board opens from a file, and is picked in the
   workspace (M5); it gets no fourth card.
9. **The project is named after its file** (`Night light.mlab` → Night light), as the student sees it in their
   folder; a new project is "Untitled project" until it is saved.
10. **Start screen height (N-13):** the board cards grow with the window, and their pictures scale to fit, so there is
    no empty band below them at 1440 or 1920; the Tip card sits at the foot of the side column. Recent projects (F-05)
    take the space under the cards in M5.
11. **E2E flake:** in one full run, one test failed and I didn't capture which — before the fix it was the divider
    restart test. The fix: the e2e helper now waits until the window has drawn before it checks for the start screen.
    After that, 120 runs in a row passed (`--repeat-each 8`).
12. **Where things are is in `project.json`** as an optional `view` (the board's and each breadboard's corner, in
    drawing units: 16 = 0.1 in). A file without it gets the default layout, so the format stays 1. Parts and wires
    have no positions of their own — they are drawn from the holes and pins they use, so they can't miss one.
13. **A hole holds one thing** — one leg or one wire end, as on a real breadboard — and so does a board pin. A part
    is refused when a leg would land on a used hole, off the breadboard or on a board pin; the canvas says why.
14. **In M4 parts go only in breadboard holes.** Parts plugged straight into a board's header, or left loose on the
    canvas, are not in v1's plan; the micro:bit GPIO board plugged into the breadboard (`plugs`) comes with M5's
    full breadboard.
15. **Clicking a part in the panel adds it** in the first free place (column by column from the left), so the
    keyboard can add parts too; the push button is always put across the channel, so its two sides aren't joined
    by the breadboard.
16. **Wires are straight and coloured by what they join** — black to ground, red to a supply, the others in turn —
    until wire colours and bend points (F-13, M5). The drawing is fitted to the canvas when a project opens; zoom,
    pan and fit come in M5 (F-17).
17. *(3b)* **Clicking a part no longer drops it in a free place** (finding 15 is replaced): as in Tinkercad, a click
    picks it up and a click on the breadboard puts it down. The keyboard keeps a way in: Enter picks it up at the
    first free place, and the arrows move it.
18. *(3b)* **A click near a hole ends a wire** (within 10 units, about two-thirds of a hole's spacing), as in
    Tinkercad; a bend is a click further from every hole. Bends, the board and the breadboards are on an 8-unit grid
    (half a hole), so wires run straight along the rows and columns.
19. *(3b)* **The inspector floats at the canvas's top right** (screen 02b shows it next to the part); it closes when a
    wire or a part is started, so it never sits over a click, and with Esc, × or a click on empty canvas. A new part
    or wire is selected, as in Tinkercad, so its value or colour can be set at once.
20. *(3b)* **Wire bends are where they were drawn.** Moving a breadboard moves the bends of wires with both ends on
    it; a wire from the board to the breadboard keeps its bends. Moving and deleting single bends is M5 (F-13).
21. *(3b)* **The full breadboard is small at 1440 wide** (63 columns fitted in the canvas): zoom and pan (F-17, M5)
    fix it; until then the parts panel or code panel can be made narrower.
22. *(4)* **CodeMirror 6 without its `basicSetup`**: only what F-20 needs now (Python, line numbers, history, bracket
    matching, indent). Autocomplete per board is M5 (F-20, screen 02b); search, folding and lint marks are not
    planned. Its undo is the code's own; undo / redo for the circuit is M5 (F-16).
23. *(4)* **The LED's look goes beyond the approved prototype** (the teacher's request): + / − badges, the bent anode
    leg and the flat edge. The prototype's screens are not redrawn; the app is now the reference for the LED.
24. *(4)* **A bug found by a stronger test:** the canvas's Delete / Backspace used `isContentEditable` to leave typing
    alone, which is not reliable; it now looks at where the key came from (a field, or the code editor).
25. *(4)* **An e2e test raced the first render** (the start screen measured before it was drawn — the app is larger
    with CodeMirror): it now waits for the heading. Three runs in a row, 60 / 60 passed.
26. *(5)* **The simulator runs in Electron as designed** (architecture §2.2): a module worker per run, MicroPython
    WebAssembly and the circuit inside it, Stop and button presses through the shared buffer. The e2e tests drive it
    for real; Stop returned well inside N-03's 0.5 s on every board.
27. *(5)* **The micro:bit has no `boot.py`** (its firmware runs `main.py` only, `codal_port/main.c`): the simulator ran
    one on every board until now; it no longer does on the micro:bit.
28. *(5)* **While running, the circuit is the program's**: nothing can be added, moved, wired, deleted or set in the
    inspector until Stop (as in Tinkercad); the parts panel says so. A push button is pressed by holding the mouse on
    it. A knob's position while running comes with M6's controls (F-35); until then it stays where the circuit has it.
29. *(5)* **After the run**: Stop darkens the parts and the board (the student stopped it); a program that ends by
    itself leaves them as it left the pins, as a real board does, until the next run.
30. *(5)* **The console keeps the last 200 000 characters**, so a program printing in a fast loop can't fill the
    memory; it follows the end unless the student has scrolled up. The REPL is M9.
31. *(5)* **Not yet:** the line being run marked in the editor (screen 03), hovering a part for its current and
    voltage, and friendly explanations of errors (F-24) — M9's work, as planned.
32. *(5)* **A slow test made fast:** putting a part in a free place rebuilt the list of every hole for each place it
    tried (6.5 s to fill a breadboard under coverage, a timeout); holes are now looked up by position (60 ms).
