## Modelling decisions (slice 1)

1. **What is typed goes as a board's serial console takes it**: a ring of characters in the Control buffer (after the input slots), read by the worker; `\x03` in it never reaches the text but raises KeyboardInterrupt (`mp_hal_set_interrupt_char`), what was typed before it stays. Text typed while nothing reads waits for the next `input()`, as on a board.
2. **The REPL runs in the session's worker**: the worker tells the program's end (`ended`) and goes on taking lines until Stop; Stop at the REPL ends the worker at once (nothing runs to stop). It keeps the circuit the program started with, so a changed circuit ends it.
3. **Timers and IRQs go on at the REPL and in input()**: the wait is the board's sleep, as after main.py on a board (no soft reset until Ctrl+D).
4. **The window's banner, not the board's**: after the program, the console says *main.py ended. Its names are still here…* (screen 29c) instead of the board's `MicroPython v1.29.0 on …; Type "help()"` lines.
5. **Explanations never replace MicroPython's message**; they are keyed on its exact texts and say nothing for an error they don't know (the student's own exceptions, KeyboardInterrupt). Branches for messages MicroPython never prints ("expected an indented block", "can't convert 'int' object to str implicitly") were dropped.
6. **"More about NameError"** (screen 05's second button) waits for M8 Learn, which it links to.

## Findings (slice 1)

1. A line running at the REPL prints no prompt: the console's field was named after the prompt shown and lost its name mid-line (the e2e test found it). It is now named for the session (the program, or its REPL).
2. The canvas note took `role="status"`, which the status bar has: it is a `note`.
