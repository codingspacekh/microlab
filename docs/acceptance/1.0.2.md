## Findings and open questions

1. **Not compared with the real thing by me.** The firmware's C was read, never compiled or run. What the port says
   was checked by reading it beside the C and by hand-traced tests. **Gate, for the teacher**:
   - `npm run golden -- --board microbit-v2` records `tests/golden/microbit-v2/08_speech.py` on the real micro:bit —
     the phonemes of 27 phrases and SAM's errors (it makes no sound). The golden test then compares the simulator's
     with them, phrase by phrase.
   - Listen: the same `speech.say(...)` in the simulator and on the board.
2. **On a pin, a buzzer hears the voice's pitch, not the words** — the simulator's pins carry a pitch, not samples.
3. **Where the C reads past the end of an array** (three places that ordinary speech reaches: the flags of the end
   marker, the phoneme before the first, a division by zero in a pitch transition) the port does what the
   micro:bit's memory layout and processor do; the choices are commented where they are made.
4. **Very long phoneme strings** (120 or more phonemes) can make the firmware's own loops run past its buffers; the
   port stops instead. `say()` is limited to 80 characters by the firmware, as here.
5. `sing()`'s notes are now SAM's own (1.0.1's 15 300 / pitch Hz was an estimate and is used only for the pin).
