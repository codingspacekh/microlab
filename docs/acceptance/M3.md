**Mutation checks.** Slice 1: the pins not rechecked after a solve → 2 fail (the press's IRQ and the micro:bit's
`was_pressed`). Slice 3: PWM as its DC average only → 3 fail. Slice 4: a servo moving with no power → 1 fails.

## Findings and decisions

1. **Solve on demand.** A write only marks the circuit as changed; the solve happens at the next read, ADC, sleep
   or VM hook (every 64 backward jumps). A run of writes with nothing reading costs one solve, which keeps the
   ≥ 10 000 writes a second of the gate within reach, and a fault (M3.5) is still found within a few
   microseconds of simulated time.
2. **An output pin reads its register, not the solved voltage**, so the trace stays what the program drove. (On a
   board, a pin shorted to GND reads 0; here the short is a fault that stops the run, M3.5.)
3. **Inputs scripted onto a pin** (M2's `inputs`) still win over the circuit, so the M2 tests run as they were.
4. **PWM is averaged over its states, not as a steady `duty × voltage`** — this refines the wording of §1.7 ("its
   average (duty × voltage)"), for the teacher to confirm. Taken literally, an LED on 50 % PWM would get 1.65 V,
   stay below its 2.0 V forward voltage and stay dark; on a board it glows at half brightness. Solving the circuit
   with the pin high and with it low and weighting the two by the duty gives that half brightness, and is the
   same as duty × voltage for resistors (M1's test still holds). Faults look at every state, so an LED with no
   resistor burns at 10 % too, as a real one does on its peaks. The servo's pulse width (also §1.7) comes with the
   part states (slice 4).
5. **Part states — choices to confirm:** an LED's `brightness` is its current over the 20 mA rating (0.29 for
   5.9 mA); how bright that looks is the window's drawing (M4). The servo's range is SG90's common 0.5–2.5 ms; it
   moves only with ≥ 4.5 V across it (M1's servo rule) and starts at 90°. The Pico W's LED is on the Wi-Fi chip,
   not in the circuit: its state is the pin trace's `LED`.
6. **Blink with the resistor taken out ends as `led-burnt`** (the pin saturates at its 48 mA; the LED takes 30 mA
   at most) — the "over-current fault" of the M3 test list. The pin's own over-current is a warning in M1's rules
   (the pin protects itself by saturating); the damage is the LED's.
7. **Fault messages are plain and technical** (`led1: the LED burnt out — 48 mA through it, it takes 30 mA at most`).
   The friendly wording, the picture and the fix are M9's fault table (screen 04).
