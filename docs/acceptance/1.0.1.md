## Findings and open questions

1. **Not tried on a real board by me.** The Stop fix is tested against the fake board (a program deaf to the first
   Ctrl-Cs, one deaf to all, a Stop in the middle of the file's transfer). **Gate: the teacher tries Stop on a real
   micro:bit and Pico W** — at once after Run, during a long program, in *Both at once*.
2. **After a Stop the board is restarted softly.** Its names are gone at `>>>` (as after Thonny's Stop). A pin left
   high by `Pin.value(1)` stays high on a Pico W / ESP32 — a soft restart does not reset pins. A program that ends by
   itself is left as it is (a `Timer` it started goes on).
3. **A program with a bare `except:` in its loop cannot be stopped over USB** (Thonny neither): after about 5 s the
   run ends in MicroLab and the console says to unplug the board.
4. **speech: the voice is a stand-in.** The firmware's voice is SAM (Software Automatic Mouth, 1982; `lib/sam` in
   micropython-microbit-v2). Its source has no licence, so it was not copied. In the simulator each letter is a short
   buzz at the voice's pitch — one hears that it speaks, when, how fast, how high (`sing()` plays its notes), not the
   words — and `translate()` raises `NotImplementedError` ("only on the real micro:bit"). **For the teacher to
   decide**: (a) keep the stand-in; (b) port SAM (the real voice and `translate()`; listed under About ▸ Licences as
   having no licence); (c) the computer's own voice says the words (clear, but not the micro:bit's voice, and
   `pronounce` / `sing` stay as now).
5. **Estimates in speech**: a letter lasts 80 ms at speed 72; pitch p sounds at 15300 / p Hz (from the docs' table
   for `sing()`: 115 = C … 58 = the next C). To check against a real micro:bit.
6. `NeoPixel` keeps its brackets (one constant, ORDER; it is only ever made).
7. Four icons already in `design/build.py` for M13 (exam, key, upload, user) came into `icon-data.ts` with the
   export of the two new ones.
