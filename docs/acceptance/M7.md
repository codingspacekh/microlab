## Modelling decisions (slice 1)

1. **A driver is switches, not a formula.** Each output of an H-bridge is two switches (to its supply, to GND), a relay is its coil and two contacts, a ULN2003 channel is one sink. Its inputs are read from the solved voltages, so a driver works from whatever drives them — a pin, a rail, another module — and with PWM it is solved in each on / off state, as the LEDs are (M3.3).
2. **The L298N's drop is its switches' resistance** (≈ 5 Ω each): on 5 V a TT motor gets ≈ 3.6 V (≈ 130 rpm, not 180). Its logic needs 4.5 V: from its 5V screw, or from its 12V screw through its regulator while the 5V-EN jumper is on — a simplification: the real regulator's output on 5 V in is lower; its 5V screw is not driven as an output here.
3. **A motor on a pin is damage** when the pin drives it with more than its safe current: the pin gives all it can (48 mA on the Pico W, 15 mA on the micro:bit), and a real one is hurt by that and by the motor's kick. The card of screen 25c (with *Show me how*) is M9's fault table, as screen 04's; until then the message is in the console.
4. **A stepper follows its coils**: a new pattern a half step or a full step from where its rotor is moves it (either way); a jump of more it can't follow. No speed limit yet (the patterns are read when the circuit is solved, so a program's writes between two steps are not each a step).
5. **A servo turns at 600°/s** (an SG90's 0.1 s per 60° at 4.8 V) and draws ≈ 200 mA while it turns, ≈ 10 mA at rest — so VBUS's budget is real (screen 25's status bar).
