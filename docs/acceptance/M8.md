### Decisions made while building (say if any should be otherwise)

1. **Five numbered sections, not seven.** The approved pages (30b, 30c) number five: the heading says *what it is*, and *its legs* are in **Its numbers** (the LED) or with **how to connect it** (the potentiometer). 2. **The Sensors are on Learn's home and in its list from the start**, each marked *soon* until slice 8.4 — screen 30 showed them as a pill under *More parts*. The six big ideas of Draft 2 are listed the same way (screen 30 showed three).
3. **The boards are not listed yet** (Draft 2 moved their pages to later); screen 30's list had them.
4. **A right-click on a part with no page shows no menu**, rather than a menu with nothing to choose.
5. **The parts panel's way in is a second button beside the part**, shown on hover and reached by Tab — the part itself stays one button to drag.

## Slice 8.2 — the basic parts (built 2026-11-16)

*Started on the teacher's word, 2026-11-16 ("yes continue"). The words are the draft's ([`M8-pages-basics.md`](M8-pages-basics.md)), not yet corrected by the teacher: each page is a text file in `library/learn/parts/`, so a correction is a change of words there.*

| Part | Look inside: what is moved → what is seen | Its numbers from the simulator |
|---|---|---|
| Breadboard | A hole chosen (a click, or the Row and Column sliders) → its strip lights, the wire in it; **Lift the cover** shows every strip and rail | which holes are joined (`strips`), five to a strip |
| Resistor | The resistance slid along the common values, 47 Ω – 10 kΩ → the stripes change, the current through the loop and the LED after it: dim, bright, too much, burnt (**New LED**) | a 6 V pack, the red LED's 2 V, 20 mA and 30 mA |
| RGB LED | Three sliders, red, green, blue → three chips in one case, the colour your eye sees and its name | each chip's volts (2, 3, 3 V), 10–20 mA, its resistors on 5 V (220, 150, 150 Ω) |
| Push button | Held (the mouse on the picture, or **Press the button** with Space) → the dome comes down on the two contacts, the LED lights; from above, the two pairs of legs | which legs are joined, always and while pressed (the model's) |
| Slide switch | Slid left or right → the bridge joins the middle leg to leg 1 or leg 3, one LED or the other | which legs each position joins (the model's) |
| Light sensor (LDR) | The light slid from dark to sunlight → rays, loose charges on the track, its resistance; with a 10 kΩ resistor on 3 V, the volts between them | its resistance by the light (`ldrOhms`): 1 MΩ, 15 kΩ, 3 kΩ; 0.4 V and 2.3 V |
| Buzzer | Active or passive; the pitch slid, 200–4000 Hz → the waves close up, the pushes in a hundredth of a second, the note's name | the active one's 2300 Hz and 2 V, the passive one's 1 V swing |
| Battery pack | 2 × AA, 4 × AA or 9 V → the cells in a row, their volts adding; what it feeds slid, 5–500 mA → how long it lasts | 3, 6 and 9 V; 1.5 V a cell |

Each picture is its own file (`src/renderer/learn/figs/`), drawn with the shared pieces of `fig-kit.tsx`, and has its own tests (`tests/component/learn-fig-*.test.tsx`, 85 in all). The glossary has five more words: Ohm's law, frequency, hertz, common cathode, common anode.

### Decisions made while building (say if any should be otherwise)

1. **Only *Look inside* has a picture**; *Connect it* is words (the LED's page alone draws its circuit, as approved on 30b). A drawn circuit for each part can follow if wanted.
2. **Two numbers are not the simulator's**, which has none for them: an AA cell holds about 2000 mAh and a 9 V battery about 500 mAh (`BATTERY_MAH`), and the LDR is read with a 10 kΩ resistor — each kept in one place for the page and its picture.
3. **The buzzer's picture is silent**: it shows the note, it does not play it.
4. The lists of the draft's *Its numbers* became tables of three columns, as on the approved pages.

## Slice 8.3 — the big ideas and the glossary (built 2026-11-16)

*Asked for by the teacher, 2026-11-16 ("can you do the IDEAS section next"). The words are a first draft for the teacher to correct — each page is a text file in `library/learn/ideas/`, the glossary is `library/learn/glossary.md`.*

An ideas page (screen 30d): its heading, **1 Play with it** — the picture — and **2 The rule** in its box, then a table of where it is met, and beside them the page's own **Glossary**: the lines of the words it uses.

| Idea | Play with it: what is moved → what is seen | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage, current and resistance *(30d, approved)* | The push and the narrowing slid → water round a loop of pipe: the pump, the narrow place, the flow; under it the same loop as a circuit | Current = push ÷ holding back: I = V ÷ R |
| A circuit is a loop | Two switches and a wire, each opened or closed → the flow all the way round and the LED lit, or no flow anywhere | No loop, no current |
| Inside a breadboard | The current followed a step at a time through a small circuit; the cover lifted → the strip of metal it is in at each step | Same strip, same point |
| Series and parallel | Two lamps joined one after the other or side by side; either taken out → each lamp's volts and brightness, the battery's current | In series, parts share the push; in parallel, each gets all of it |
| Digital and analog | A voltage slid from 0 to 3.3 V; the number of steps chosen → a digital 0 or 1, the level, and its number on a staircase | Digital has two levels; analog has every level |
| PWM | How long it is on in each blink slid; slow or fast → the pulses, what they add up to, the LED's brightness | On for part of the time looks like part of the brightness |

**The glossary** now has its 35 words (F-42) and the two for an RGB LED's shared leg: one or two lines each. A page underlines a word; an ideas page shows the lines of its own words.

**The potentiometer's page** has its link to *Voltage, current, resistance* (screen 30c) — the fourth thing found in slice 8.1.

### Decisions made while building (say if any should be otherwise)

1. **A digital reading is 1 from half the supply up** (1.65 V of 3.3 V) — the simulator's own rule for a pin. Real boards differ a little; the page names no board.
2. **The lamps of *Series and parallel* are plain resistances** of 100 Ω on a 6 V pack, so the sums stay round (30, 60, 120 mA); the simulator has no lamp.
3. **The pictures do not move by themselves**: the water's flow, the pulses and the blinking are drawn, not animated — what is drawn follows the sliders.
4. Every ideas page has *The rule* and a picture: the checker asks for both, and refuses a board's name here too.
