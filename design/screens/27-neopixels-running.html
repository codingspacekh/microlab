27 · NeoPixels, running (M7 design, slice 3) — a NeoPixel stick of 8 and a NeoPixel ring of 12 (WS2812B) lie above the breadboard, wired at their pads like the displays' headers: DIN, 5V, GND in, and DOUT out to chain more; the arrows printed on the stick show the way the data goes. Each LED shows the colour write() sent it — np[i] = (r, g, b) and fill() set them, nothing changes until write() — taken bit by bit from the pin's 800 kHz bitstream (machine.bitstream), 24 bits an LED in the chip's own order (green, red, blue), the first 24 kept, the rest passed on. A dim LED is drawn in its full colour with a smaller glow, as the eye sees one at an eighth. neopixel is built into MicroPython on all three boards: no library file. Hovered, an LED says its number, its colour and its current; the status bar adds them up against what USB gives (500 mA): each LED draws up to 60 mA at full white. The ring's inspector: a stick (8, 16 or 30) or a ring (8, 12, 16 or 24), LED 0 at DIN's end / at the top, the rest clockwise. Both on VBUS (5 V); the knob on GP26 sets how bright.
