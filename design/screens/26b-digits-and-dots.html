26b · Digits and dots, running (M7 design, slice 2) — a 7-segment digit (common cathode) straddles the gap, its body over its ten pins (their names printed at its edges): seven pins drive a–g, one 220 Ω on its COM to GND — the lit segments share that resistor's current, so a 1 is brighter than an 8, as on the real one (one resistor a segment keeps them even); the kit's 4-digit display (TM1637) shows numbers() with its colon, and an 8×8 dot matrix (MAX7219) on SPI0 shows the dots pixel() set, when show() sends them, at its brightness(). Both modules' headers stick out of their sides, so they lie beside the breadboard and their pins are points to wire; the matrix's second header (DOUT) chains another. The matrix on VBUS (5 V), the TM1637 on 3V3. The drivers from the library pack (tm1637.py, max7219.py, both MIT)
