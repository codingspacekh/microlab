28b · Bluetooth, from a phone (M7 design, slice 5) — an HC-05 Bluetooth module lies below the micro:bit's GPIO board, its header up: EN VCC GND TXD RXD STATE, points to wire — VCC on 5V (it has its own 3.3 V regulator), the micro:bit's TX (P1) to its RXD, its TXD to the micro:bit's RX (P0): uart.init(baudrate=9600, tx=pin1, rx=pin0), any two pins. The phone card is a phone paired with it: Connect, then what you send arrives on the micro:bit's UART, what it sends shows as a reply. Its LED blinks fast until a phone connects, then twice every 2 s; STATE is high while connected. 9600 baud, as the HC-05 comes. On the micro:bit, uart.init() with pins takes the USB console away (print() goes nowhere) until uart.init() with none, or the program ends — the console says so. The JDY-31 is the same module to the program (its inspector's Kind). The HC-05's AT commands (EN high as it powers up) are not modelled yet
