Board

Raspberry Pi Pico W

A small computer on one board — the microcontroller. It runs one program, and its pins switch things on and off and read what's joined to them. The W means it has Wi-Fi.

1What's on it

2Its pins

Power out · 3V3 (3.3 V), VBUS (5 V from USB) GND · 8 of them, all joined GP0–GP22 · on, off, or reading GP26–GP28 · can also measure voltage

A GP pin is like a tiny switch the program controls: on gives 3.3 V, off gives 0 V. Or it can listen and tell the program whether something joined to it is high or low.

3Its limits

3.3 Vthe most a pin can take. 5 V into a pin damages it.
12 mAthe most a pin should give: an LED, through its resistor.
50 mAall the pins together.
300 mAfrom 3V3 for your parts.
5 Vfrom USB at VBUS, for a servo or a motor driver.
3 ADCpins that measure 0–3.3 V.

4Where its power comes from

The USB cable brings 5 V. A small part on the board, the regulator, turns it into the 3.3 V the chip needs — and that is what comes out of 3V3 for your parts.

5Mistakes

5 V into a pinThe pin takes 3.3 V at most: a real pin would be damaged. Bring a 5 V signal down with two resistors first.
A motor straight on a pinA motor wants far more than 12 mA: use a motor driver.
