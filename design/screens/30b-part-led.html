LED

A tiny light. It glows when a small current flows through it — but only one way round. LED means light-emitting diode.

a power lighttraffic lightstorchesscreens are millions of them

1Look inside

Current through it
15 mA
At 15 mA it shines bright. The current comes in at the long leg, crosses the tiny chip — that's where the light is made — and leaves by the short leg. Slide past 30 mA and the chip gets too hot: it burns out, for good.

2The idea

An LED is a one-way door for current. Push from the long leg and the door opens — the chip lights. Push from the short leg and it stays shut: no current, no light. The door needs a small push to open: about 2 volts for a red LED. Its colour comes from what the chip is made of, not from the plastic.

4Connect it

Always with a resistor in the loop: it holds the current back to a safe size. Battery + → resistor → long leg; short leg → battery −.

Which resistor? Take the LED's 2 V from the battery's volts, then divide by the current you want (0.015 A):

3 V battery68 Ω
5 V supply220 Ω
6 V (4×AA)270 Ω
9 V battery470 Ω

A bigger resistor is safe — the LED is just dimmer. Never smaller than the table says.

5Mistakes

No resistorFar too much current: it flashes bright and burns out. MicroLab burns it too, and says why. See it happen
The wrong way roundThe one-way door stays shut: it stays dark. Nothing breaks — turn it round.
Both legs in one columnThe breadboard joins them, so the current goes round the LED, not through it.
currentHow much electricity flows past a point each second. Measured in amps (A); small currents in milliamps (mA): 1000 mA = 1 A.
