13b · Board chip: every state — what the chip says, when, and what a click on it shows (F-55, F-56, §1.13); the colour says whether something is needed: none · checking · ready · busy · needs you · can't connect

{{include:topbar-simple}}

No board

Nothing plugged in, or nothing the app knows.

Click: “Plug a micro:bit, Pico W or ESP32 in with a USB cable that carries data.” Help connecting…

Checking

For up to 2 s after a board is plugged in: the app knows its USB chip and asks it for MicroPython's greeting.

Nothing to do. The board's own program carries on afterwards.

Ready

The board answered, and it is the project's board.

Click: the board's card — version, space, files; Send, Console, Board files (screen 13).

Sending · running on the board

While files go over, then while its program runs with the console open (screen 16).

Wrong board

The board answered, but the project was built for another board: its pins and modules differ.

Click: Plug in a Pico W, or Change this project's board to ESP32 (the wiring is checked again).

Used by another program

The port is there but another program holds it — named when the app can tell (Thonny, Arduino IDE, mu, another MicroLab window).

Click: “Close Thonny, or press Stop/Disconnect in it.” Try again.

No MicroPython

A micro:bit that runs a MakeCode program, an ESP32 with Arduino code, or a program that won't stop.

Click: Install MicroPython… (screen 14b; on a micro:bit its files are copied off first).

Firmware mode

The Pico was plugged in with BOOTSEL held: it shows the RPI-RP2 drive and runs no program.

Click: Install MicroPython 1.29… (screen 14b), or “Unplug it and plug it back in without holding the button.”

Can't connect

A known USB chip with no port (a missing driver on Windows), a port that won't open, or a board that stopped answering.

Click: the connection helper, at the step that failed (screen 14).

Two boards

More than one board plugged in.

Click: the list, each with its state; the one chosen last is chosen again next time.

Plugged in · out

{{i:usb}}micro:bit plugged in — MicroPython 2.1.2. This project is for a Pico W.
{{i:usb}}Pico W unplugged. The project is still here; plug it back in to send.

A short message at the bottom right for 5 s; the chip changes at once.
