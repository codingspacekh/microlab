28 · UART, running (M7 design, slice 5) — a USB-serial adapter (CP2102) lies below the Pico W, its header up: RXD TXD GND (and 3V3, 5V out), points to wire — the board's TX to its RXD, the board's RX from its TXD, GND to GND. Its terminal card is what a serial monitor on the laptop shows: what the board sends, and a line to send it (with its line ending). Bytes go as the wire would carry them: a start bit, 8 data bits, a stop bit, at the baud rate — 9600 baud is about 1 ms a byte; they wait in the board's receive buffer (any(), read(), readline()), as long as it is. The terminal's baud rate and line ending are in the adapter's inspector, and on the card. The adapter's TXD and RXD LEDs flash as bytes go. The status bar counts them. Wiring TX to RX between two of the board's own UARTs works too (a loopback); the ESP32's UART0 is its USB console, as on the real one
