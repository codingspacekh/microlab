29 · The multimeter (M9 design) — Meter on the canvas's tools takes out a multimeter: its card, and two probes — red (+) and black (COM) — dragged onto any hole, pin or terminal; it reads what is there, as a real one: V the voltage from the black probe to the red (here the middle of two 10 kΩ from 3V3: 1.65 V, half of 3.3); A the current through a wire or a leg clicked (as if the meter were put in its place, in series; mA and µA); Ω the resistance between the probes with the power off, as a real meter needs (both resistors from 3V3's column to GND: 20.0 kΩ; a probe on each end of one: 10.0 kΩ). It reads with the board on USB as it is — a program running or not (its pins are inputs then); while one runs it follows the program (a PWM pin reads its average, as a real meter's does). Off the end of its range it says OL. Esc or Meter again puts it away; the probes stay where they were for the next time
