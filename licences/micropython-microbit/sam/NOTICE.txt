SAM — Software Automatic Mouth

The micro:bit's speech module (speech.say, pronounce, sing, translate) is SAM, a speech synthesizer first published
for home computers in 1982 by Don't Ask Software (later SoftVoice, Inc.). The micro:bit firmware carries a C version
of it in lib/sam (microbit-foundation/micropython-microbit-v2), which comes from the reverse-engineered C conversion
of the original program published at github.com/s-macke/SAM.

That source carries no licence. Its publisher states that the original program is long out of print, that the
company no longer exists, and that the conversion therefore cannot be put under an open-source licence.

MicroLab contains SAM in two places:
  - compiled inside the micro:bit firmware it can install (micro:bit MicroPython v2.1.2, unchanged);
  - in the simulator, as a line-by-line TypeScript port of the firmware's lib/sam (src/sim/sam), so that a program
    that speaks sounds in the simulator as it does on the board. Its tables are copied byte for byte.

It is included for teaching, as the micro:bit itself includes it. All rights in SAM remain with their owners.
