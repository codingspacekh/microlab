Electricity only does something when it can go **all the way round**: out of the battery, through the parts, and back to the battery.

## Play with it
figure: circuit-loop

## The rule
**No loop, no current.** Current needs a whole path from the battery's + back to its −. Open the loop anywhere — a switch, a loose wire, a burnt-out part — and it stops everywhere at once.

## Why everywhere at once
The battery does not send current out and hope it arrives. It pushes the charges that are already in the wire, all round the loop at the same time — like a bicycle chain. Stop one link and the whole chain stops.

That is why a switch works wherever it is in the loop: before the lamp, or after it.

## What a loop is made of
| A push | the battery | without it nothing moves |
| A path | wires and parts | metal all the way: a [conductor] |
| Something to do | a lamp, a buzzer, a motor | it also holds the current back — without it the loop is a [short circuit] |
| A gap, when you want one | a switch or a button | air is an [insulator]: it opens the loop |
