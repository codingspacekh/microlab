# The glossary

One or two lines a word, for students of 10–13 (F-42). A page underlines a word by writing it in square brackets.
The first words: those the first pages use. The full list comes with the ideas pages (M8 slice 8.3).

## current
How much electricity flows past a point each second. Measured in amps (A); small currents in milliamps (mA): 1000 mA = 1 A.

## voltage
The push that makes a current flow, like the pressure that pushes water through a pipe. Measured in volts (V).

## resistance
How much something holds the current back. Measured in ohms (Ω): more ohms, less current for the same push.

## ground
The − side of the circuit, where the current goes back to the battery. Boards mark it GND. Every voltage is measured from it: ground is 0 V.

## short circuit
A path from + straight to − with nothing to hold the current back. Far too much current flows: wires get hot and batteries empty fast.

## voltage divider
Two resistances one after the other, sharing the [voltage] between them. The point in the middle gets a part of the push — a bigger part the bigger the resistance below it.

## Ohm's law
The rule that ties the three together: [current] = [voltage] ÷ [resistance]. More push, more current; more resistance, less.

## frequency
How many times a second something happens again — a shake, a blink, a wave. Measured in [hertz].

## hertz
The unit of [frequency], written Hz: 1 Hz is once a second, 440 Hz is 440 times a second.

## common cathode
An RGB LED whose shared leg is the − side of all three chips: it goes to the battery's −.

## common anode
An RGB LED whose shared leg is the + side of all three chips: it goes to the battery's +.

## circuit
A loop the [current] can go round, from the battery's + back to its −. Break the loop and it stops.

## volt
The unit of [voltage], written V. A AA cell pushes with 1.5 V.

## amp
The unit of [current], written A. One amp is a lot for a small circuit: an LED takes about 0.02 A.

## milliamp
A thousandth of an [amp], written mA: 1000 mA = 1 A. Small circuits are measured in milliamps.

## ohm
The unit of [resistance], written Ω. 1000 Ω is 1 kΩ.

## power
How fast energy is used: [voltage] × [current]. Measured in [watts = watt]. More power means more light, more sound — or more heat.

## watt
The unit of [power], written W. 1 V pushing 1 A is 1 W.

## series
Joined one after the other, in one path: the same [current] goes through each part.

## parallel
Joined side by side, each on its own path: each part gets the whole [voltage].

## conductor
Something current goes through easily: metals, like the copper in a wire.

## insulator
Something current cannot go through: plastic, rubber, glass, air. A wire's coat is one.

## polarity
Which way round a part goes: which leg to + and which to −. An LED and a battery have polarity; a resistor has none.

## anode
The + leg of an LED: the long one.

## cathode
The − leg of an LED: the short one, by the flat edge.

## forward voltage
The push an LED needs before it lets current through: about 2 V for red, about 3 V for blue.

## digital
Having only two levels: on or off, high or low, 1 or 0.

## analog
Able to be any level, not just on or off: the [voltage] from a knob, or from a light sensor.

## signal
A [voltage] that carries a message — "the button is pressed", "it is 21 degrees" — rather than power.

## pulse
A short time on, between two times off.

## PWM
Pulse-width modulation: switching on and off very fast, and changing how long each [pulse] stays on, to dim a light or slow a motor.

## duty
In [PWM], how much of the time it is on: 25 % is on for a quarter of each blink.

## sensor
A part that measures something — light, heat, distance — and turns it into a [signal].

## actuator
A part that does something you can see or hear: a motor, a buzzer, a light.

## pull-up
A resistor from a [pin] to +, so the pin reads high when nothing else is pushing it — an open button, for one.

## pin
A leg of a board or a chip that a wire goes to.

## bit
One [digital] answer: a 1 or a 0. Ten bits together can count 1024 steps.
